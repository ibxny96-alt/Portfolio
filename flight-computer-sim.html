Sensor fusion

The raw accelerometer angle is noisy and jumpy; the gyro drifts slowly over time. The complementary filter blends fast gyro response with the accelerometer's long-term accuracy to get a clean estimate close to the true attitude.

How the pipeline works

Stage 1

Sensors read the world

The gyroscope measures how fast the craft is rotating; the accelerometer estimates tilt from gravity's direction. Both are cheap, both are noisy, and neither alone is trustworthy.

Stage 2

The filter fuses them

A complementary filter integrates the gyro for fast, smooth response, then slowly corrects that estimate toward the accelerometer so drift doesn't accumulate.

Stage 3

The controller reacts

A PID loop compares the fused estimate to "level" and computes a corrective output proportional to the error, its rate of change, and its accumulation over time.

Stage 4

Motors correct the craft

That output becomes differential motor thrust, tilting the drone back toward level — which the sensors then measure again, closing the loop, hundreds of times a second.

This is a self-contained software simulation of the estimation and control logic used in real quadcopter flight controllers (e.g. Betaflight, PX4). The physics, sensor noise, and gains are simplified for clarity — a real build runs the same complementary-filter and PID logic on an actual MCU reading an actual IMU over SPI.
