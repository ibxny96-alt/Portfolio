About this model

This tool solves the 2D, incompressible, inviscid limit of the Navier–Stokes equations — the potential-flow equations — exactly, via a Joukowski conformal transform calibrated to the requested NACA thickness ratio, rather than by discretizing the airfoil into panels. That avoids the numerical instabilities that naive panel-method implementations are prone to, at the cost of being restricted to this airfoil family (camber is added back in through classical thin-airfoil theory as a zero-lift-angle shift).

Lift & pressure (inviscid)

The airfoil shape is treated as the conformal image of a circle (z = ζ + 1/ζ). Flow around the circle — uniform stream + doublet + a bound vortex — has a closed-form complex potential; circulation is fixed by the Kutta condition (smooth flow off the trailing edge). Lift follows exactly from the Kutta–Joukowski theorem, L' = ρV∞Γ, and surface velocity/Cp come from dW/dz evaluated on the mapped boundary. Streamlines are integrated in the simple circle plane, then mapped through the same transform.

Drag & stall (viscous, semi-empirical)

Potential flow alone predicts zero drag (d'Alembert's paradox), so CD is built from Reynolds-number-dependent flat-plate skin friction (Blasius/Schlichting) with a thickness form factor, plus a lift-dependent profile-drag term. This is a 2D (infinite-span) analysis, so there is no 3D induced drag. Stall is approximated with a smooth empirical roll-off past a Reynolds-dependent critical angle, in the spirit of engineering post-stall extrapolations — it is not a resolved boundary-layer separation.

On "real" CFD: fully resolving the Navier–Stokes equations (a meshed, iterative finite-volume solve — e.g. OpenFOAM, SU2) captures boundary-layer separation, turbulence, and deep-stall behavior that this model only approximates, but it can't run interactively in a browser — a single case typically takes minutes to hours. A natural next phase for this project is to run such a solver offline across an angle-of-attack sweep and compare its CL/CD polar against this potential-flow model, to map out exactly where the inviscid+empirical approximation breaks down.
